# OpenHK50 Android design

- **Today** opens on an activity dial and its goals. The open arcs and matching labels use apricot for band calories, lavender for workout time, and mint for steps. Distance sits beneath the dial. Yesterday’s steps and last night’s sleep each open their daily histories. Seven-day averages use the previous seven complete local dates with readings.
- **Navigation** groups Today, Workouts, Health, and Band in a floating Liquid Glass-inspired bar on phones and a rail on wide windows. Keep surfaces translucent with a mint-tinted rim and a distinct selected state, and keep content legible below them. History returns to the originating tab. Camera remote and Phone clock use dedicated views.
- **Motion** slides and fades between tabs and detail views in the direction of travel. History periods, pop-ups, and clock mode changes use short vertical transitions. The activity dial animates from its displayed progress to new readings. Routine refreshes do not replay screen transitions. Honor Android’s animation setting.
- **Copy** starts with useful data or the primary action. Give each destination one navigation label. Omit repeated tab titles, greetings, and decorative weekday or date headers. Use dates where they identify a history range or workout record.
- **Appearance** follows Android’s theme. Dark uses a midnight indigo canvas (`#10101B`) and lighter indigo panels. Light uses a lavender-white canvas (`#F3F2FB`) and white panels. Match history charts to their metric colors. Use rose for heart measurements, cyan for oxygen, blue for sleep, and distinct activity icons in the workout picker and records. Keep native system bars, permissions, back handling, and font scaling.
- **Data** uses calories from the band’s activity packets and workout minutes from completed phone sessions. Workout time excludes pauses and is apportioned to the local days it crosses. Personal builds show estimated active calories on live and saved Strength sessions. Empty readings display a dash. History retains day, week, and year drill-down and keeps spot checks separate from historical averages.
- **Band** starts with connection and sync, followed by nearby devices during discovery, tool tiles, alerts, settings, and backup. Phone vibration offers app search and per-app switches after notification access is granted. The tested HK50 has no screen or button. The phone provides the digital and analog clocks and workout controls. The wrist shutter works while Camera remote is open through the confirmed camera-mode event. See [HK50 hardware and protocol evidence](docs/reference/HK50.md).

## Apple references

- Use the floating navigation and color guidance from Liquid Glass with Android’s system controls and accessibility settings. [Liquid Glass materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)
- Fitness puts daily progress, steps, distance, recent sessions, and trends in Summary. Apple’s Stand metric requires detecting standing and movement by hour. OpenHK50 uses steps as its third goal. [Fitness Summary](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/guide/iphone/iph4c34a8a95/ios)
- Apple groups workouts by activity and provides pause, resume, and end controls. Sleep exposes the previous night, history, and stage breakdowns. [Workout guide](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/guide/watch/apdd16e8761a/watchos) · [Sleep guide](https://support.apple.com/en-md/guide/watch/crpxel1lvun9/watchos)
- Apple’s Watch companion manages devices, faces, and app arrangement. On the watch, the screen and Digital Crown handle navigation. OpenHK50 keeps device tools together and lets users switch the phone clock between digital and analog. [Watch setup](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109015) · [Face Gallery](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/guide/watch/apda6559ad78/watchos) · [App arrangement](https://support.apple.com/en-my/guide/watch/apd3cd8641c2/watchos) · [Watch controls](https://support.apple.com/en-us/105063)
- Apple’s Camera Remote uses the watch to frame and trigger photos. OpenHK50 uses the phone preview with a wrist shutter, optional three-second countdown, and phone camera selection. [Camera Remote guide](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/guide/watch/apda6e61c287/watchos)
